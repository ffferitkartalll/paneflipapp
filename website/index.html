PaneFlip

Split your Mac's screens into zones. Drop apps into them, and flip between them from each zone's notch.

Download for macOS View on GitHub
brew install --cask ffferitkartalll/tap/paneflipapp

Free and open source (MIT). Needs macOS 27.

How it works

Three ideas, and your windows always know where to go.

1

Snap

Drag a window while you hold ⌃⌥. The zones light up; let go over one and the window slides in and fills it. Apps you put in a zone are remembered and return when they reopen.

2

Stack and flip

Several apps can share one zone. Point at the zone's notch and click a window, scroll over it, or press ⌃⌥← → to flip.

3

Go beyond the screen

A layout can go on past the edges of the display, with as many screens as you like. Press ⌃⌥⌘← →, scroll over the island in the menu bar, or swipe.

Made for the way you work

Layouts you draw, windows that stack, and a Mac that remembers.

Snap

Drag a window into a zone

Hold ⌃⌥ while you drag and the zones light up. Let go over one and the window slides in and fills it, or drop it on a zone of the island in the menu bar. PaneFlip remembers which app belongs where and puts it back when the app reopens. Dragged out of its zone, a window gets back the size it had.

  • ⌃⌥ hold while dragging Show the zones
  • let go Fill the zone

Notches

A notch on every zone

Every zone that holds a window has a small notch at its top edge. Point at it and it grows, a part for each window, the one in front lit. Click a card to bring its window forward, drag the cards into another order, scroll over it to flip, or press the "more" button for the cards of the island. The cards show the Dock's notification badges.

  • point Grow the notch
  • click a card Bring it forward
  • Flip, no keys

Stacks

Many apps, one zone

Put more apps in a zone and they stack. The keys flip the zone with the focus: the next window glides in beside the one it replaces, and the zone's notch shows where you are.

  • ⌃⌥→ Next window
  • ⌃⌥← Previous window

Scroll

Scroll through a zone’s windows

Hold ⌃⌥ and scroll over the zone with two fingers: the windows follow your fingers, pass several at once and settle on the nearest one. A mouse wheel steps one window at a time. Or choose three fingers in Settings, with no keys at all. The speed is a setting.

  • ⌃⌥ + scroll Through a zone’s windows
  • Two fingers

Keyboard

Send a window to a zone

Press ⌃⌥M and the island opens with your zones. The arrow keys choose one, on any display; ↩ puts the focused window there, ⌫ takes it out of its zone, esc cancels.

  • ⌃⌥M Open the island
  • ↓ Choose a zone
  • ↩ Put it there

Keyboard

Move the focus, enlarge a window

Four keys on the places of an inverted T move the focus to the zone above, on the left, below or on the right, on to the next display too. ⌃⌥↩ enlarges the focused window to the whole screen for fifteen seconds; it goes back to its zone when the time is up, or when you press it again.

  • ⌃⌥L Zone on the right
  • ⌃⌥K Zone below
  • ⌃⌥↩ Enlarge

Beyond the screen

Screens past the edge

A layout can go on past the edges of the display, with as many screens as you like. Press the keys, scroll over the island in the menu bar, hold ⌃⌥⌘ and scroll, or swipe with four fingers, and the whole layout slides over and comes to rest at the next screen. A window that gets the focus there brings its screen into view.

  • ⌃⌥⌘→ Screens to the right
  • ⌃⌥⌘← Screens to the left
  • ⌃⌥⌘ + scroll Slide the layout
  • Two fingers

Layouts

Draw your own layout

Click to split a zone, drag a line to resize, drag a zone onto its neighbour to merge. Make a grid of equal zones with two sliders, or start from one of 21 common shapes. Keep a zone at an aspect ratio, star the zone new apps go to, and set the gap, the margin and the smallest zone for each layout.

  • click Split
  • drag a line Resize
  • drag a zone Merge

Menu bar

Switch layouts in one click

Every desktop of a display can use the layout you choose for it. Pick another from the menu bar and the windows glide to their new zones.

  • Menu bar Choose a layout

And everything around it

The details that make it feel like part of the Mac.

The layout editor

  • Click to split a zone, hold ⇧ to split it across. A line sticks at ¼, ⅓, ½, ⅔ and ¾; double-click its handle to remove it.
  • Grid: move the Rows and Columns sliders, watch the zones, then Apply.
  • Spacing: the gap between zones, the margin at the screen's edges and the smallest zone, for each layout. Equal shares make equal zones.
  • Keep a zone at 1:1, 4:3, 3:2, 16:10, 16:9, 21:9, or standing up: its other lines move with the one you drag.
  • Add a screen at either edge with a plus, undo and redo, name layouts and zones, duplicate, export and import a layout.

The island

  • Point at the island in the menu bar: your zones drawn small, and buttons to edit the layout, open the selected apps, Settings and Quit.
  • Click a zone for its windows as cards. Quit, close, hide or minimize an app, take it out of its zone, or tick it to open with the layout.
  • Drag a card into another order, onto another zone, or out of its zone. Drop a dragged window on a zone of the island.
  • Double-click the card in front to enlarge its window. The island hides while an app is full screen.

Zone edges on the desktop

  • Press ⌃⌥ twice quickly: a handle shows on every line between the zones of the screen.
  • Drag a handle and the windows follow. The lines reach the menu bar, the Dock and the screen's sides.
  • The island takes a move back with its Undo button; Done or esc ends it.

Undo

  • Put a window in a zone or take it out by mistake? ⌃⌥⌫ undoes it.
  • The island says what happened in one line, with an Undo button, for ten seconds; ⌃⌥esc closes it.

Trackpad and mouse

  • Two fingers with the keys held, or, with no keys, three fingers for a zone's windows and four for the screens beyond the edges.
  • Over the open island and over a notch, the wheel and two fingers work with no keys.
  • The speed is a setting, and follows the system's own trackpad speed.

Displays and desktops

  • Every desktop of a display can use a layout of its own.
  • Drag the displays to where they stand on your desk: the focus, the windows and the pointer go from one to the next as they stand.
  • Unplug a display and its windows spread over the others; plug it in and they go back to their zones.

Stacks

  • Choose which app opens in which zone and open them all with one click.
  • Closed, hidden and minimized windows leave their zone and return when they do.
  • Apps you never want in a zone go on the list of ignored apps.

Everywhere

  • Every shortcut can be changed in Settings.
  • Make Caps Lock a modifier key and every shortcut gets a version on ⇪; capitals are then ⌘⇪.
  • Launch at login, and one switch that turns everything off without quitting.
  • Updates from inside the app or with Homebrew. 46 languages.
  • Layouts and settings stay on your Mac.

Install

PaneFlip asks for two permissions on the first launch, Accessibility and Screen Recording, and tells you why.

From a download

Open the .dmg of the latest release and drag PaneFlip into Applications. The app is signed with its own certificate, not an Apple developer one: the first time, open it, then choose System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Open Anyway.

Get the latest release

With Homebrew

Homebrew removes the quarantine mark, so no extra step is needed.

brew install --cask ffferitkartalll/tap/paneflipapp

Or take the quarantine mark off by hand:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/PaneFlip.app
